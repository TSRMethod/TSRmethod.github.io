Why do four triangles share one BioTSR key?

Ubiquitin (PDB 1UBQ), chain A, computed with the protein Cα method of BioTSR 1.0.0. Four different triangles in it have the same key, 17637781. Choose one, rotate the structure, and see what changes and what stays the same.

Drag the picture to rotate it; scroll the page beside it. Pinch to zoom.

With the picture focused: arrow keys rotate, plus and minus zoom, 1 to 4 choose a triangle, R resets the view.

A key keeps only three things: the three vertex labels, which bin the triangle’s angle θ falls in, and which bin its longest edge falls in. Exact positions and lengths are not part of it, so different triangles whose values land in the same bins get the same key.

θ is the angle between the base (the first two vertices in BioTSR’s order, highest label first) and the line from the base’s midpoint to the third vertex, folded into 0–90°. In the picture the dashed line is that median and the bold edge is the longest edge.

The four triangles

All four have the vertex labels Val 20, Ile 10 and Leu 8, θ in class 11 and maxDist in class 5, so all four get key 17637781, and the .keys file gives that key a frequency of 4. Their positions in the chain and their exact angles and lengths differ.
